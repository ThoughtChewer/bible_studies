## Read through: [Genesis 4.1-4.26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=genesis%204&version=NASB)
- Question: Big picture, what is happening in this story?
	- Just let them talk.
- Question: What are interesting things that you noticed?
	- Just let them talk.
- Question: Does any of this sound familiar?
	- This story parallels the stories from [[Genesis 2.4-2.25]] and [[Genesis 3.1-3.24]].
	- Reference image for after manually digging through the text [[Genesis 2-4 Parallels.png]].[^1]
- Question: If this story is a parallel, who represents the two different groups, the seed of the woman and snake?
	- Cain and Abel are seeds of the woman.
	- Cain becomes seed of the snake when he gives into sin.
	- This tells us that the seeds are not solely a lineage thing, but instead based on choices.
- Question: Are there any structural elements that you can identify to the story?
	- The story opens and closes with Eve giving birth.
	- The story seems to be centered about Eve's children, though there is a section about Cain's children in the middle.
- Question: What do you notice about the description of Eve the bookends the story?
	- In verse 4.1, Eve is still struggling with wanting to be in control. She claims to have created a man with Gods help, which implies that she is equal to God. 
	- When Seth is born in 4.25 Eve has a much more humble tone when she says that God gave her a son.
- Question: What is the emphasis of Cain's portion of the story and how does the author communicate that to the reader?
	- The important part is Cain's response to God.
	- The technique that the author uses to communicate this is the proportional time spent discussing that compared to everything else.
- Note about Cities:[^2]
	- In the ancient world, cities are a collection of homes surrounded by a wall for protection.
	- The point at which a town becomes a city is when the walls are built.
	- Cities are run by kings.
- Question: What is Cain doing symbolically by building a city?
	- He is taking protection into his own hands instead of trusting God.
	- Hebrew wordplay: The word for city is [ʿîr (עִיר)](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5892/esv/wlc/0-1/). Which is almost the same word as when God "builds" a helper [ʿēzer (עֵזֶר)](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5828/nasb20/wlc/0-1/) in [[Genesis 2.4-2.25]]
		- Cain is attempting to bring about his own salvation (ezer).
- Question: Lemech is king backwards in Hebrew. So Lemech is king of the city, and are often a representative of what the culture is like. So what is Lemech like?
	- Instead of having one wife to be unified with, he has multiple like something to be collected.
	- “While Cain, Lemek’s ancestor, would be avenged seven times over by God, Lemek takes it into his own hands to satisfy his desire for vengeance through a seventy-sevenfold retaliation. At this point, the principle of retributive justice is pushed to the point of becoming the caricature of itself. Moreover, there is a profound irony in Lemek’s effrontery to refer to the divine protection of his ancestor Cain. As Lemek’s ‘immunity,’ by contrast, is at the cost of unlimited violence, he has turned the divine grace to Cain into its ugly counterfeit ... Any trivial inconvenience is occasion for Lemek to yield to violence. What he demands is not an eye for an eye, but a life for a wound. Lemek‘s song is the last spasm, so to speak, of Cain‘s murder story ... bringing the horror of fratricide to its apex" [^3]
- Question: This is the first city we have encountered in the story, and is going to begin an architype of cities. How would you describe the city?
	- Founded on fear by people who want to build their own salvation.
	- They are places of advancement and new technologies.
		- Not inherently a bad thing.
		- Though in 4.22, tools of bronze and iron usually end up creating weapons which aren't great.
	- Full of violence and oppression. (see note about Lemech above)
- Question: What kind of effect do cities have on the surrounding environment?
	- Make the connection between Lemech killing people and the description of Abel's death.
	- There is a tainting effect on the earth, and the cries of the deceased rise up to God.
## Conclusions:
- People are now congregating in places based on fear of their neighbors. In an effort to create their own salvation they becomes places of violence and oppression that taint the earth and cry out to God.
	- This is what it looks like when mankind has found their own good instead of trusting God for the good.
- At the end of 4.24, the reader is left with a dark picture of humanity that lies in stark contrast to the image presented to the reader in [[Genesis 1.1-2.3]] wondering if there is any hope of us ever becoming who God created us to be.
- Then the story concludes with a ray of hope. Eve has had a change of heart, and with the birth of her new son, people have started to turn back to God.
	- This is another theme that will continue through the story, there will always be a remnant that provides a ray of hope.
- We have a parallel story right afterwards because this is representative of the human condition. We are creatures of habit and repeat cycles in our own lives. Additionally, frequently people inherit the characteristics of their parents which lead to generational cycles.
	- It also gives the author a chance to expand upon the story by giving us different perspectives.
- Regardless of which side you are born on, each generation will choose which side they are going to be on through their actions.
	- Cain started as the seed of the woman, and decided to become the seed of the snake.
	- As we can see from how Cain's descendants turn out, that choice will heavily impact the next generation, but they will still have the ability to choose.
	- You can also see it in Eve's response to giving birth correlated with the children the birth produced.
### Questions for the future:
1. If humanity is becoming this bad, is God going to do something about it?
2. We know what the seed of the serpent looks like (Lemech), but what does the seed of the woman look like?

### Practical things to talk through with the learner:

	- Relationship with Dad frequently shapes how we view God.
	- Cultural values get passed on.
- Are there things from your parents that you want to imitate?
- Are there things from your parents that you want to leave behind?
- Based on the choices you are currently making, which seed do you think you are more aligned with?
