## Read through: [Genesis 10.1-10.32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2010&version=NASB)
- Question: Big picture, what is happening in this story?
	- Just let them talk.
- Question: In [[Bible Study 6 - Line of Seth]] we talked about genealogies having interesting tidbits that give you clues as to what is happening. Do you see any interesting bits?
	- 10.5 - "separated into their lands, every one according to his language, according to their families, into their nations."
		- Strange choice of words.
		- Follows God's command to populate the earth from 9.7.
	- 10.8 - Story about Nimrod.
		- Stories give us clues to the meaning.
	- 10.21 - Out of place line about Shem and his great-grandchild Eber.
	- 10.25 - Eber's first son has a description of his name.
	- 10.30 - Description of Eber's second son's settlement.
	- 10.32 - Genealogy concludes by saying that the nations were separated all over the earth.
	- If you counted, there are 70 names listed in the genealogy.
- If you recall previous discussions from [[Bible Study 2 - Creation]], 7 represents completeness. The 70 listed people are telling the reader that all nations have their origins from Noah.
	- So part of 10.5 is telling us that people are spreading out in their family groups and making nations.
- In Hebrew, your name was kind of like your destiny. Like Adam means mankind and Eve means life. As a result, people's names will often provide clues to the meaning of the story.
	- Nimrod
		- We have discussed many of the places mentioned in conjunction with Nimrod back in [[Genesis 2.4-2.25]] with the rivers. Bad things come out of these places.
		- Nimrod has also become the icon of a great warrior. He has made a name for himself.
		- [Nimrod] in Hebrew means "rebellion" or "the valiant"
			- So his name characterizes both aspects of the description that we are given, and we can expect the next time he shows up in the story that that section of story will be related to his name in some capacity.
	- In 10.25 when it talks about why Eber named Peleg, [Peleg](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h6389/nasb20/wlc/0-1/) literally means division in Hebrew.
		- We know that Shem is the line that God is going to focus on in the biblical story, but Peleg tells us there is a division between him and his brother.
- Question: We are expecting a "chosen" and "not chosen", but which is Peleg and which is Joktan? And how do you know?
	- Joktan is "not chosen". The clue is that Joktan settled in the east.
		- We haven't talked about it yet, but the learner may have picked up on it (discussion below).
	- If they don't get it have them read [Genesis 11.1-2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2011.1-2&version=NASB).
		- They should connect east to Shinar (11.2), and Shinar to Nimrod (10.10).
- Every time there is a rebellion against God, the people involved go east
	- [Genesis 3.24, 4.16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%203.24%2C%204.16%2C%20&version=NASB) , and chapter 11.
