When you translate from one language to another you have to make many choices. The main choice you make is if you are going to translate word-for-word or thought-for-thought. Word-for-word doesn't always capture cultural or implied meanings where as thought-for-thought misses key hyperlink phrases used to navigate the bible. One is not better than the other. They have their tradeoffs, and it is helpful to read multiple translations of the bible as you study it to gain a better overall understanding.

Regardless of which translation you select, there are going to be issues. So learning some Hebrew words and how to use a concordance will be very helpful in understanding what the bible is trying to communicate. Sometimes the English word just doesn't property replicate the idea to the reader. [[Bible Study 3 - Adam and Eve]] has a great example with Eve being characterized as a "helper" in English. Whereas the Hebrew is more akin to "the solution to your problem that you could not fix yourself". Then when God "builds" Eve out of Adam's side, that is the same word used when Cain builds a city in [[Bible Study 4 - Cain and Abel]], and city just happens to be almost the same word as "helper" in Hebrew. So Cain is trying to build his own deliverance instead of relying on God. But all of that is completely missed in the English translation.
