## Discussion
- You may want to go through [[Bible Study Appendix A - Jewish Content in the Bible]]
- Reflecting on Eden [[Genesis 1.1-2.3]] [[Genesis 2.4-3.24]]
	- Think about [[Genesis 1.1-2.3]] as an ideal and the overarching story of the bible. God creates a world where Heaven and Earth are united where he has delegated rule of his domain to others, and they live in harmony creating together for all eternity (day 7 doesn't end).
		- The place where Heaven and Earth meet is a temple. God wants all of his creation to be his temple, represented by him creating it in [[Genesis 1.1-2.3]].
	- Then in [[Genesis 2.4-3.24]] you expect to meet a real version of the humans described in [[Genesis 1.1-2.3]].
		- Adam was created outside the garden, and put into it (Genesis 2.8). He was allowed to go into God's temple.
		- Adam and Eve don't live up to the idea that was presented. Its not that everything was perfect and then they ruined it. It was that the ideal was never realized.
	- But God seem intent on making humans part of the plan despite of their short comings.
	- There is this lingering thought God said Adam and Eve would die, yet they are still here.
- Reflecting on Cain and Abel [[Genesis 4.1-4.26]]
	- We discussed how the city was a place of fear (the walls), and it was ruled by a blood thirsty man (Lamech is king backwards in Hebrew). So implied is the city is full of distortions of God's creation.
		- Lamech took 2 wives --> women are not part of the unity between man and woman that represents God (Genesis 1.27), but something to acquire and collect.
		- The insecurity of man is leading him to kill more people, and their blood is soaking the earth and crying out to God (ref Genesis 4.10).
	- That section of the story ends, and the reader is left thinking "What hope does humanity have to get back to the Garden and be the people God created us to be?" 
	- Then there is this little paragraph giving us a seed ;) of hope. Adam and Eve have another child, and people being to call upon the name of the lord.
		- So we have an image of what the seed of the serpent looks like (in Lamech), but what does the seed of the woman look like? (ref Genesis 2.15)

## Read through [Genesis 5.1-32](http://biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%205&version=NASB)
- You are not alone in thinking this is boring, monotonous, and not important. Most Americans do.
- Yes there is another person named Lamech, but this one is a different person.
- There is clearly a pattern that is repeated, but if you paid attention, you'll notice the pattern breaks. These breaks give us insights into what the passage is about.
	- Genesis 5.21-24 - Enoch didn't die. The only clue we are given is that he walked with God.
		- Genesis 5.5 - Adam eventually did die.
		- This gives us further insight into the story of [[Genesis 2.4-3.24]]. 
			- God is the ultimate source of life. He gives it freely to everyone, the good and the evil 
				- ref Genesis 2.10-14 - River flowed from Eden to places that include Babylon, Assyria, Egypt, and Cush. If you have some bible knowledge, you know bad things are happening in those places.
			- But in the disconnected state where Heaven and Earth are linked, but not completely joined, humans are still not completely connected to God, and they eventually die.
	- Genesis 5.29-31
		- Lamech tells us more about Noah
			- Noah's name means rest in Hebrew
			- Somehow Noah is going to undo the curse from Genesis 3.17
	- Genesis 5.32
		- Noah's entry also breaks the mold.
		- concludes in 9.28-29 with sections in: 6.9-10, 7.6.
		- This tells you that the flood story is also part of the genealogy, and will provide more insights.
- This leaves the reader expecting that Noah is the guy we've been waiting for. He is going to set things right and get us back into the garden.
