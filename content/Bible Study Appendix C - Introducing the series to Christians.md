
Something familiar must become foreign before it can become new. If you have been around the bible for any significant amount of time the only way to make it foreign is for me to have a potentially very uncomfortable conversation with you. I hope the following will invigorate your desire to read the bible and grow closer to God.

![[angels.png]]
Above are two pictures of angels, but you didn't need me to tell you that. You could just glance at the picture and know that they are angels because of what they look like. There is just one problem... Angels in the bible don't have wings. 

Ok, that's a cool factoid, but does it really matter? It's not like how I envision an angel has any barring on my relationship with God. You're right, how you envision an angel doesn't really matter. But look at the images above again, what happens if you remove the wings? The one on the left goes from being an angel to being a woman with a dress blowing in the wind, and on the right goes from cupid to a kid with a toy bow and arrow. So the identity of the being you are looking at completely changes.

This is was the very problem the Jews had when Jesus arrived. They thought the messiah would look like the one on the left, and they got the one on the right... So not only did they miss him, they killed him.

![[jesus.png]]

I grew up in the church and became a Christian in my teens. As a young Christian I thought how could the Jews have been so blind? It was right there and for whatever reason they just didn't want to see it. Eventually it clicked for me that I was no different than the Jews. The passages in the bible I was so familiar with, I realized I didn't really understand. And the man I had been trying to follow for most of my life was shaped more by the culture I lived in than the book written about him, just like angels. Scot McKnight was a professor at a seminary. On the first day of his Jesus class he would give the students a psychological test. He would ask them to answer the questions based on how they think Jesus would answer the questions. Then he collected the tests, and gave them another test and asked them to answer the questions about themselves. The second test has the same questions, but they are worded slightly differently. Then he would compare the results of the two tests. Scott found that most students describe Jesus the same way that they describe themselves. He states that, "Spiritual formation experts would love to hear that students in my Jesus class are becoming like Jesus, but the test actually reveals the reverse: Students are fashioning Jesus to be more like themselves."[^1]

I think one of the main reasons is that we don't know how to engage with the Bible the way that it was designed to be engaged with. We have deceived ourselves into thinking that just because we have read the entire book and understand all the words because they are in English that we know what is going on. Let me give you an example.

When you read Genesis, how much time do you spend thinking about [Genesis 11.27-32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=genesis%2011.27-11.32&version=NASB)[^2]? Most people I know skim it really quickly or skip it to get to the more important story about Abram immediately afterwards. If you fall into that camp, you are likely treating the bible like a history book or an instruction manual, and if you treat it that way you will completely miss the point of the scriptures. That has nothing to do with your intelligence. The authors designed the material in such a way that unless you know how to engage it you can glide right through it and not realize just how much you missed. 

For anyone who has stopped to think about this passage, did you realize that this is the creation story? If your response is something like, "God didn't create anything here. What do you mean this is the creation story?" There is a big difference between what happens in a story and what it means. And you, likely, either lack the skills to find the meaning or haven't spent enough time wrestling with the passage to get there. This is where I was. I had no clue how much I didn't know till someone opened a window for me.

Regardless of where you are at in your journey, developing the skills to engage the bible on its terms is not beyond you. I have created this series to help you learn and practice those skills so that you can deepen your relationship with God and join him in his mission. I pray that the material helps and that God will bless your efforts.
